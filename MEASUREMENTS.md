# MirrorDash — Measurements

The measurements and checks behind decisions in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), kept apart so the decisions stay short. Each part names the decision it belongs to. They are records of what was seen on a date, on that hardware and software: measure again before relying on one for a new decision.

## Video on a Pi 3

Behind ARCHITECTURE #32 (a lean GStreamer in the image, video played outside the browser) and #33.

* **Video must be fullscreen**: only then does wlroots put the decoded frames straight on a display plane (the Pi's hardware scaler does the scaling, as in Kodi) instead of compositing them with the mirror's page on the GPU. Measured on the Pi 3B: SVT 540p, 720p and 1080p at 50 fps, 0 dropped, fullscreen (the plane shows `YU12` in `/sys/kernel/debug/dri/0/state`); in a window, 540p dropped about one frame a second and 720p reached 35–40 fps. Decoding alone (`fakesink`) holds 50 fps at 720p, so the decoder was never the limit. A 50 fps stream on a 60 Hz screen still jerks every fifth frame: switch the screen to 50 Hz while playing (`wlr-randr --mode …@50Hz`, only a mode the screen lists, and back afterwards).
* **Video in a window** is composited by labwc on the GPU, and that holds 50 fps only while the page behind is still: a blank page dropped 2 frames in 40 s, but a page that changes one number a second drops about one frame per change (39–55 in 40 s, the clock's seconds on the mirror page gave 26). Every repaint of the page takes the Pi 3's GPU long enough for labwc to miss a video frame. Painting the page on the CPU (`WEBKIT_SKIA_ENABLE_CPU_RENDERING=1`) brought it to 15–21; WebKit's damage features (`--features=+PropagateDamagingInformation,+UnifyDamagedRegions`) made no clear difference. labwc doesn't put windows on display planes (wlroots' `wlr_output_layer` is unstable and unused), so a smooth window needs the page to hold still while a video plays. Weston 14.0.2 (Trixie) does put windows on planes: tested in RAM on the mirror (2026-10-20), with the desktop shell, the video window (`YU12`, 960×540) and Cog's page each got their own plane, and the ticking page cost 6–8 dropped frames in 35 s instead of 39–55. Its desktop shell crashed at once in the plane code (`drm_output_find_plane_for_view: Assertion 'fb' failed`) with a plain background colour, and ran with a background image. Weston has no `wlr-randr` (rotation, resolution and screen off at runtime) and lets no client place its own window. What decides it under labwc is how much of the page the GPU redraws, and at what resolution. Measured on 2026-10-21 with the real mirror page (only the clock, with seconds), SVT1 at 360p in a quarter-width window in the bottom right corner, 90 s each: 1920×1080@50 dropped 67 frames (1.5 %), 1280×720@50 (the monitor scales up) dropped 5, and 1920×1080@50 with the clock's seconds off dropped 11 (0.25 %). The whole Pi used 8–15 % CPU and stayed at 60–62 °C in all three, so the limit is the GPU's time, not the CPU. The window is placed with a labwc window rule on its app id, which is the program's name (`gst-launch-1.0` for gst-launch; `g_set_prgname` sets it in a player of our own), not a GStreamer name; without a matching rule labwc centres the window at the video's own size. A player must take only the video and audio from SVT's playlist: with the subtitle rendition left unlinked, `uridecodebin3` sometimes hung in preroll.
* **Video inside the page** works once WebKit can reach the decoder. Tested with the sandbox off (`WEBKIT_DISABLE_SANDBOX_THIS_IS_DANGEROUS=1`, a few minutes, our own page; 2026-10-20): `<video>` with hls.js used `v4l2h264dec` and dropped 0 frames at 540p, 720p and SVT1 1080p, also with a number ticking on the page. The web process used 60–80 % CPU (120 % at 1080p) and the Pi reached 75 °C. SVT2 at 1080p dropped 31 %, so 720p is the safe level. With the sandbox on, the decoder isn't visible and WebKit decodes in software. The reason: WebKit 2.48 (Trixie) binds only `/dev/video0`–`video2` and `/dev/media0` into the sandbox, and the Pi's decoder is `/dev/video10`. WebKit binds every V4L2 node from 2.52 on ([bug 311007](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=311007); 2.54 is in forky, not in Trixie or its backports). Loading the decoder as `/dev/video0` (`bcm2835_codec decode_video_nr=0`) is enough: tested with the sandbox on, WebKit then used `v4l2h264dec`. The frames that come late show only with `requestVideoFrameCallback` (`getVideoPlaybackQuality` said 0 dropped while a viewer saw the hitches): at 720p, gaps over 45 ms came about every two seconds with a still page and about once a second with a number ticking each second. Putting the number on its own layer, changing it right after a video frame, or painting on the CPU didn't remove them. The web process used 75–88 % CPU at 720p, most of it the hls.js/MSE path (75 °C). WebKit's own HLS (no hls.js) is off in 2.48 unless `WEBKIT_GST_ENABLE_HLS_SUPPORT=1`, and then takes only the old `hlsdemux` (plugins-bad), not `hlsdemux2`, also with `WEBKIT_GST_USE_PLAYBIN3=1`. With it, and with `deinterlace`, `videofilter` and `audiofx` added (`Missing element 'deinterlace'` otherwise), SVT played at about 8 % CPU on `v4l2h264dec`, but the picture stayed on its first frame while the time ran on; without the audio and subtitle renditions too. That path is a dead end on 2.48. Sound still needs a tap: WebKit pauses video that unmutes by itself.
* **Sources and sound**: SVT's channels come from `https://api.svt.se/video/ch-svt1` (`svt2`, …): HLS without DRM, geo-blocked to Sweden, every variant from 252p to 1080p at 50 fps, with audio and subtitles as separate renditions. Sveriges Radio is a plain MP3/AAC stream (`https://www.sverigesradio.se/topsy/direkt/srapi/132.mp3` redirects to `live1.sr.se`; about 8 % CPU). Set the volume on the hearing scale (`amixer -M -c Headphones sset PCM 35%`): the raw scale's 20 % is −81 dB, silent. The admin page's volume does that on every card (`_apply_volume`): plain `amixer sset` reaches only card 0, which on the Pi is HDMI. Measured on the mirror (2026-11-03): the jack (`Headphones`, card 1) has 8 subdevices mixed by the VideoCore, so several modules play at once; HDMI (`vc4hdmi`) has one, no mixer controls (volume in GStreamer) and takes `hdmi:CARD=vc4hdmi,DEV=0` or `plughw`. It plays without error to a screen without speakers (the S24D300's ELD has `sad_count 0`), so a module can't tell it's silent. The volume lives in the RAM root and is lost at a restart. The Pi 3's 3.5 mm output is PWM and a little noisy.
* **Other compositors and strategies**: Set-top boxes running WPE (RDK, Metrological) use hole punch: the page draws a transparent hole at the `<video>` and a platform sink (`westerossink` under the Westeros compositor) puts the picture on a plane under it, at the rectangle WebKit passes it. Trixie's WPE 2.48 has it built in (`WEBKIT_GST_HOLE_PUNCH_QUIRK=westeros`), but Westeros isn't in Debian (not tried). Kodi owns DRM itself and puts its menus and the video on separate planes, which is why it plays smoothly. Cage is wlroots like labwc, so it costs the same, and it shows one client at a time. Pure DRM (`cog -P drm` beside `mpv --vo=drm`) can't work: only one process can be DRM master, and the screen can then only be turned off over CEC. vc4 rotates planes only 0°/180°, so on a portrait mirror (90°) video always goes through the GPU, whatever the compositor (not measured).

### Video in the page and in an own window, WPE 2.54 (2026-11-13)

Pi 3B, test version `v0.16.0-os2`, WPE WebKit 2.54.2, GStreamer 1.26.2, labwc 0.20.2, screen 1920×1080@60, the normal mirror page running, 720p50 H.264 clip with AAC audio unless said otherwise. CPU is in % of one core (4 cores). A gap in the page = a frame more than 45 ms late (`requestVideoFrameCallback`), counted per 30 s run; a gap in the own window = a cluster of 2 or more frames the sink dropped, after the first 3 s, given per 10 s. The two counts are not the same measure. The kit's limits are 60 % CPU and 1 gap per 10 s. Reports: `results/2026-11-13-root-diagnosis.md` and `results/2026-11-13-planb-own-window.md` in the mirrordash-tv-testkit repo.

**`<video>` in the page.** The decoder is `v4l2h264dec` (hardware) and every frame reaches WebKit's sink (about 1383 samples in 30 s), but with the default settings the card stays black on the compositor's screenshot while the page reports 48 fps (1 run). With `WEBKIT_GST_DMABUF_SINK_DISABLED=1` the video is painted (screenshot and camera, 3 runs). The default path hands over DMABuf `YU12` planes; the switch makes WebKit upload them to a GL external-OES texture. Where the default path loses the frame is not shown (no log at any level checked).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| switch set | 3 | 25.3 (23–28) | 141.1 (139.0–142.5) | 47.1 (45.4–48.2) |
| switch + `WEBKIT_GST_USE_PLAYBIN3=1` | 3 | 19.7 (14–26) | 134.0 (132.0–135.1) | 45.4 (44.6–47.0) |

* Dropped frames (`getVideoPlaybackQuality`) were 0 in every follow-up run. Earlier single runs with the switch gave 33 and 39 gaps and 144–145 % CPU; without it 28–55 gaps and 126–142 %.
* Playbin3: CPU about 7 points lower (ranges do not overlap); gaps not shown to differ (ranges overlap); first frame later (1.9–3.3 s against 1.1–2.8 s).
* Audio track: 22 points less CPU without it (2 runs); the page's `muted` does not remove the audio chain. Gaps did not follow.
* Threads in one run (% of a core): page main thread 20.6, compositor 19.6, GStreamer queue threads about 36, network process 8, launcher 13, audio sink 4.6, decoder 4.1. The other runs have the same shape.
* `WEBKIT_GST_DISABLE_GL_SINK=1`: painted, 1.2 fps (1 run). `WEBKIT_GST_USE_PLAYBIN3=1` alone: not painted (1 run). No software H.264 decoder is in the image.

* The page is not still: about every 12 s the screen unit jumps to 20–55 % for 1–2 s, and 55–70 % of the gaps lie on those spikes (`waylandsink` loses 20–40 frames in a row there, `glimagesink` 3–13). Beside the live page the window meets the CPU limit but not the gap limit at 50 fps.
* Fullscreen and covering windows: the screen unit falls to about 2 % (the page stops painting; the cause is inferred). At fullscreen 720p the scanout plane holds the player's `YU12` dmabuf directly (`/sys/kernel/debug/dri/0/state`, 1 run); in every windowed run the plane holds labwc's own `XR24` framebuffer. A compositor screenshot during playback itself causes a gap of 15–17 frames.
* Audio (`waylandsink`, 720p50 window): none 22–26 % total (3 runs), `fakesink` 30.6 / 31.2 (2), ALSA `hdmi:CARD=vc4hdmi,DEV=0` 39.6 / 36.3 (2), playbin3 with audio 47.9 (1). Gaps did not change. No sound was sent to the jack.
* Scaling inside the pipeline (720p to 960×540): `v4l2convert` gst 90 %, total 97–100 %, 4 sink drops (2 runs); with the format left open it chose RGB, gst 81 %, 1370 of 1470 frames dropped (2 runs); `videoscale` after the decoder failed to negotiate with DMABuf (1 run).
* Window placement: `waylandsink` opens a toplevel at the video's size, centred (1280×720 at 320,180), `glimagesink` at 800,420 (partly off screen); neither can set x,y, and `render-rectangle` did nothing. `fullscreen=true` works. A labwc window rule (position, size, no decoration, always on top) put the player exactly where told in a nested labwc 0.20.2 shown in the live session (1 run). SIGSTOP on the player leaves its window on top with the last frame, SIGKILL removes it at once, the page and `screen-kiosk` are unharmed (1 run each).
* HLS (SVT1 playlist from a local file, `playbin3`, 30 s, 1 run each): with audio to ALSA it played at 47 % total CPU and 55 drops; video only, every frame was dropped as late and no picture appeared.
* Memory over 10 start/stop cycles of a 5 s window (1 series): V3D buffers 111236 → 107556 kB, `CmaFree` 43316 → 46944 kB, `cma_alloc_fail` 0, screen unit PSS 145119 → 145279 kB, `MemAvailable` 299460 → 298156 kB; throttled 0x0 throughout.

## A module's video player

Behind ARCHITECTURE #34 (the core places a module's video window, the module plays it).

* **For a module's player** (measured 2026-10-21 on the Pi 3 with SVT1): `gst-launch-1.0` doesn't do: its window's app id is its own name, and with SVT's subtitle renditions it hung in preroll (`uridecodebin3`) or waited about 20 s before the first frame (`playbin3`). A small GStreamer player in Python works: `GLib.set_prgname(window.app_id)` for the app id, `playbin3` with `waylandsink` (`fullscreen` from `window.fullscreen`) and `flags` video+audio. hlsdemux2 holds the first frame some 18 s when the master playlist has a subtitle rendition, whatever streams are selected; with the subtitle renditions removed from the playlist (and its addresses made absolute) SVT1 starts in about 1.5 s. The player from commit 776ceb4 (`mirrordash_core/features/video/play.py`, since removed: `git show 776ceb4:mirrordash_core/features/video/play.py`) does all of this and is the starting point for a TV module.

## The module sandbox

Behind ARCHITECTURE #41 (every module instance a process and a user of its own): what was checked under the real unit and on the test mirror.

* **Under the real unit** (`scripts/sandbox-check`, 2026-10-25 and, with a folder per module, 2026-10-26): uids 70100 and 70101 with all capability sets 0 and `setuid(0)` refused, neither reading the other's data, listing the host's folder, writing the site or `uids.json`, or seeing the forkserver's `/proc`; the self-check 0 of 46 open; a module leaking memory killed by the kernel alone, three times, then paused, the others in the same processes and the unit active; uv installing as the site user. Again with a folder per module (2026-10-26): `six` installed as 70003 into a folder of its own, the links the unit's user's, an instance unable to write its own module's code, and a build backend made to attack (`scripts/sandbox-check/evil`, as 70004) refused writing another module's code, a `.pth` into its folder, a link in either site and a folder in `pkgs`; what an older shared site left (70000's) taken back and removed at the next change; the self-check 0 of 49 open.
* **On the Pi 3B** (v0.9.3-os2): the nine instances as 70100–70108 with no capabilities, the self-check 43 of 43 closed (another module's data included: the control process tells the self-check's user the folders it can't list), both Home Assistant instances reaching 192.168.1.85 through their own nftables rules, and a module taking 400 MB killed by the kernel alone (`CONSTRAINT_MEMCG`, its uid, `oom_score_adj` 500) three times, then paused and named on the Dashboard, the other eleven processes untouched.

## A systemd unit per instance

Behind ARCHITECTURE #41's choice of a forkserver over a unit per instance.

* Measured 2026-10-27 (x86-64, six instances each doing a module's work: a Jinja render, JSON, logging, an asyncio loop): a fresh Python per instance, as `mirrordash-module@.service` with `DynamicUser=` would start, has 32 MB of memory of its own (Private_Dirty, not RSS: libpython's shared pages aside), a fork of the forkserver 4 MB (9 MB on the Pi with real modules, above). So the 34 MB measured on the Pi 3B before was the process's own memory, not shared pages counted twice. Of the 32 MB, fastapi (for a module's routes) is 16 MB; an instance with only the loader, Jinja and httpx would still have 21 MB of its own, and take 1.2–1.5 s to start (here; about 4 s on a Pi 3B). For the test mirror's nine instances that is 100 to 250 MB more than now, against a 278 MB limit on a Pi 3B and 154 MB on a Pi 3 A+: still not an option. What it would buy is systemd doing the uid change (no `capset` of our own) and a memory limit per instance; the users per instance the forkserver already gives, and a limit per instance stays the open gap (Limits).

## Module host memory

Behind ARCHITECTURE #41's Limits.

* Memory, measured on the Pi 3B test mirror with its nine instances (v0.9.3-os2, each process's `smaps_rollup` in a copy of the host run as pi): 167 MB of anonymous memory in the unit, against about 75 MB in one process. Of the 92 MB more: about 50 MB is each process's own copy of the forkserver's pages it touches (an instance with no module dirties 4.7 MB, all of it pymalloc's arenas and the heap it inherited, none in new mappings: reference counts are written whenever a shared object is used, which `gc.freeze()` doesn't stop; ten processes with the control process); about 10 MB the second clock and Home Assistant instance loading their package again; 9 MB `babel` loaded by both calendar and weather (9.3 MB a process); the rest other imports each process makes of its own. Preloading the standard library's modules every instance imports, `gc.disable()` in the instances and `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2` each changed the total by less than the run-to-run spread (about 9 MB). Within the 278 MB `MemoryMax` of a Pi 3B; on a 512 MB Pi 3 A+ (30 %: 154 MB) about five instances fit. ponytail: the measured way down is a process per module package instead of per instance (here about 20 MB; instances of one package would share a user). Preloading `babel` (now the core's, for the language list) in the forkserver, its import and the mirror's language before the instances fork, made it 3 MB worse, not 9 MB better (three runs each on the Pi 3B, 156 against 159 MB): a forked instance alone had 0.5 MB of babel of its own instead of 7.8, but most likely every instance copies pages of the larger heap it inherits (not measured); the 50 MB of copied pages has no supported fix (making the forkserver's objects immortal is CPython's internals).
