# Storage check

What protects a mirror's storage partition and boot partitions (ARCHITECTURE #12 and #58), and how it was checked. `run.sh` runs `check.sh` (the real hydrate and storage-rescue on a loop device) and `boot-writes.sh` (every state a power cut can leave while `cmdline.txt` or `config.txt` is written, through dm-log-writes; `replay.py` reads each back) under Debian, as CI does on every push.

## What each check showed (2026-11-08 to 11)

Until 2026-11-11 hydrate and storage-rescue looked for the config and the backups in `/storage/mirrordash/data/` instead of the app's `/storage/mirrordash/data/data/`, and so did these checks: the emulated starts below planted their backups where the scripts looked, not where the app keeps them.

- **The built OS image in an emulated Raspberry Pi 3B** (QEMU 10's `raspi3b`, the image's own kernel, initramfs and device tree; on the side of a running mirror, so not in CI). Each start was cut off by killing the emulator, a power cut; between starts p7 was changed from outside through a loop device. With the image from 472932a: after a first start, backups planted and the first two superblocks zeroed, `e2fsck -y` took group 3's copy with stale group descriptors, cleared 121,200 inodes and left errors; p7 mounted and refused every write, hydrate stopped and the app never started. That image's fix, hydrate from cc14751 run on a copy of that very partition (real e2fsprogs 1.47.2), rescued both backups before `-y`, rebuilt it and put them back bit for bit, pi's and 0600, with the bell's mark; `e2fsck -fn` clean after. The image from cc14751, start after start:

  | Start | p7 before it | What happened |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | a new card | `repart` made p6 and p7, hydrate set up `/storage`, the app started (391 s emulated) |
  | 2 | backups planted, the first two superblocks zeroed | repaired: both backups bit for bit, a planted file kept, `storage-repair` log |
  | 3 | new backups, five superblocks zeroed (only group 9's copy left) | repaired the same way; the bell told `storage-repair` |
  | 4 | every superblock copy zeroed | made anew (a new UUID), started as a new mirror (venv from `base_venv`), `storage-rebuilt` log and its red notice on the bell's first look |

  Every partition `e2fsck -fn` clean after (a plain `-n` right after a kill reports 4 until the journal is replayed, as after any power cut). To do it again: give the device tree what the Pi firmware and `dtoverlay=disable-bt` would (`fdtput`: `/chosen/bootloader` `partition` 2 and `tryboot` 0, no `bluetooth` under the PL011 so `ttyAMA0` is a console, `status = "disabled"` on `watchdog@7e100000`, whose power-domain writes QEMU's model takes for a reset), grow the image to 8 GiB (QEMU's SD card is a power of two), and append `console=ttyAMA0,115200 systemd.journald.forward_to_console=1` to the image's `cmdline.txt`; a start to the app takes about 7 minutes.
- **`scripts/storage-check`, in CI on every push.** `check.sh`, the real hydrate and storage-rescue on a loop device with real e2fsprogs and mount: a partition just not mounted and one with its first superblock zeroed are repaired and never formatted; one e2fsck gives up on is made anew with the newest automatic and hand-made backup back bit for bit, pi's and 0600, a `storage-rebuilt` log and the bell's mark; one `-y` leaves mountable but refusing writes has its backups in RAM before `-y` runs and is made anew; one e2fsck fixed but that doesn't mount stops untouched; `mkfs.ext4 -F` refuses one in use. `boot-writes.sh`, dm-log-writes: every state a cut can leave while `cmdline.txt` or `config.txt` is written, a cut inside each multi-sector write included, read as the firmware reads it and after `fsck.fat -a`: 0 of 6 and 0 of 10 broken as the image ships; the replaced file's known broken state is found (1 of 15, 1 of 19; it must be, or the check is blind).
- **Under Debian's systemd in a container.** With `nofail` and a device slow to appear, hydrate started 3.6 s before `storage.mount` ended without `After=storage.mount`, right after it with. `systemd-fsck` on a FAT left dirty ran `fsck.fat -a` and left it clean.
- **Unit tests:** `tests/test_hydrate.py` (a partition e2fsck gave up on is made anew with its backups and a `storage-rebuilt` log; one it fixed never is; `storage-rescue` on an ext4 image with its first superblock zeroed), `tests/test_api_backup.py` (weekly, unchanged, pruned, secrets, no download, restore), `tests/test_notifications.py` (told on the first look of the start that rebuilt it only), `tests/test_audit_image.py` (cmdline.txt written in place, never without `root=`), `tests/test_gpio_helper.py` (the block keeps its size, config.txt written in place), `tests/test_ab_updates.py` (the new slot keeps or pads its block), `tests/test_visual_admin.py` (the row in Chromium).
- **Not checked:** a physical Pi with a real SD card (a real power cut, a card that loses blocks, the firmware's own FAT reading). The fixed image's rescue before `-y` followed by a rebuild in a whole emulated start: with the same damage `-y` repaired the partition both times, as how a superblock copy's descriptors have gone stale depends on the partition's past, so that path is shown on the one partition that produced it (offline) and in `check.sh`, not in a start.
